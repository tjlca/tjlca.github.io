About Us

Sustainable Engineering, Measured Against Planetary Limits

“SEED envisions engineering that grows within ecological limits — where every design decision is measured against its full cost to people and the planet, and where sustainability is genuinely, quantitatively demonstrated rather than merely claimed.”

— Vision Statement
Who We Are

An Interdisciplinary Hub for Sustainable Systems

The Sustainable Engineering and Ecological Design (SEED) Group is Washington State University Tri-Cities' hub for computational sustainability research. Anchored in the Gene and Linda Voiland School of Chemical Engineering and Bioengineering and the Institute for Northwest Energy Futures (INEF), SEED bridges chemical engineering, industrial ecology, and artificial intelligence.

The group was established to meet a growing need: rigorous, transparent methods that reveal the true environmental, economic, and social cost of energy and industrial systems — and that put those insights to work during the design phase, while alternatives are still open. SEED sits in the Columbia River energy corridor, next to Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL).

Strategic Directions

Our Three Visions

1

Research Ecosystem

SEED develops interdisciplinary research and open-source tools and frameworks that advance sustainable energy and materials systems, bridging chemical engineering, industrial ecology, and AI.

2

Community & Engagement

SEED keeps research grounded in real regional needs across the Pacific Northwest, engaging communities and stakeholders and staying attentive to energy equity and environmental justice.

3

Scholar Cultivation

SEED empowers graduate students, undergraduates, and early-career researchers to become leaders at the intersection of engineering, sustainability, and AI through mentoring and hands-on research.

What We Study

Research Areas

SEED advances interdisciplinary research at the intersection of chemical engineering, industrial ecology, and artificial intelligence — designing systems that are economically viable and ecologically sound.

01

Life Cycle & Techno-Economic Assessment

Process-based, hybrid, prospective, and dynamic LCA coupled with techno-economic analysis and absolute-sustainability benchmarks — embedding environmental impact directly into engineering design and optimization.

02

Circular Economy & Critical Materials

Dynamic material-flow and agent-based modeling for wind blades, solar PV, batteries, and plastics — optimizing end-of-life and recovery pathways for critical minerals under realistic logistics and policy.

03

AI & Integrated Assessment Modeling

LLM/RAG automation of life-cycle inventories, integrated-assessment coupling (GCAM, IMAGE), and capacity-expansion-aware emission factors so analysis evolves as technologies and the grid evolve.
