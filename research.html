SEED

Sustainable Engineering and Ecological Design Group

We reveal the full environmental, economic, and social cost of human energy and industrial systems — and use those insights to design systems that are genuinely, measurably sustainable.

Our philosophy

Process to Planet

Every engineering design decision sends consequences rippling outward — from the process floor to the planetary atmosphere. SEED is built on the conviction that optimizing within a fixed system boundary is no longer sufficient.

We expand those boundaries outward, integrating ecological systems, economic equilibrium, and AI-driven analytics directly into engineering design. The result is a multiscale view that connects molecular thermodynamics, unit operations, supply chains, regional economies, and global ecosystems within a single mathematical structure.

SEED — Sustainable Engineering and Ecological Design — captures the aim: to plant the analytical groundwork for systems that grow within ecological limits.

Signature contribution

LCA as a design tool, not an audit

Life cycle assessment rests on an elegant matrix structure that is conventionally solved as a fixed, descriptive calculation — an environmental audit performed after a system is already designed. My central methodological contribution is to reframe that structure through the lens of optimization.

By introducing decision variables directly into the LCA matrices and their governing equations, the same structure becomes a prescriptive design model: process configurations, technology choices, and supply-chain flows become variables to be optimized rather than fixed inputs. This embeds LCA within chemical process design, superstructure optimization, operations research, and supply-chain network design — generalizing and scaling the Rectangular Choice-of-Technology (RCOT) method of Duchin and Levine to far larger, multiscale systems.

The motivating belief is simple: industry needs environmental impact to inform decisions during the design phase, while alternatives are still open — not as an end-of-pipeline report.

Frameworks & tools

Open-source frameworks & tools

Foundational methods and software spanning optimization-embedded LCA, dynamic material-flow modeling, and AI-assisted sustainability analytics.

PhD framework · 2017

Process-to-Planet (P2P)

The first life cycle assessment performed directly inside an optimization language (GAMS), unifying chemical-process optimization, process-based LCA, and hybrid input–output LCA — later enhanced with a computable general equilibrium model. Validated on corn-ethanol and soy-biodiesel supply chains.

With Prof. Bhavik Bakshi

Techno-Ecological Synergy (TES)

Evaluates engineered systems against absolute planetary boundaries and ecosystem carrying capacities — introducing service-shed delineation and payment for ecosystem services. Applied to biofuel supply chains and treatment wetlands for Lake Erie algal blooms.

AI + LCA

LiAISON

LLM- and RAG-powered LCA platform on the Brightway2.5 framework, automating inventory matching, ecoinvent queries, and impact computation. Couples integrated assessment datasets (GCAM, IMAGE) for LCA under evolving technology portfolios.

Dynamic MFA

CELAVI

Circular Economy LCA & Visualization — a dynamic material-flow model tracking stocks and flows of wind-blade, PV-glass, and composite materials over time, optimizing end-of-life pathways under infrastructure and routing constraints.

Agent-based model

4P — Plastic Parallel Pathways Platform

Couples agent-based modeling and system dynamics to simulate consumer recycling behavior and quantify trade-offs between chemical recycling, mechanical recycling, and waste-to-energy for PET bottles.

Inventory & data

PyLCIA · StEWI · SESAME

PyLCIA delivers real-time dynamic LCIA on the USLCI database; StEWI unifies USEPA emissions and waste inventories on the Federal LCA Commons; SESAME (built at MIT) integrates LCA, life-cycle costing, and pathway optimization — now a commercial decarbonization-planning tool.

Where the lab is heading

Four interconnected research thrusts

A forward program that builds from inventory data, to impact assessment, to AI-driven scaling, to real-world deployment.

1 Process-Based Inventory Generation

The biggest barrier to assessing emerging technologies is missing inventory data. I generate inventories from first principles — process modeling, thermodynamics, reaction and separation analysis — combined with technology-readiness and learning-curve projections, and train machine-learning models (including neural networks constrained by first-principles rules) to screen, fill, and correct open inventories at scale.

2 Regional Impact & Absolute Sustainability

Regionalized life cycle impact assessment for North America — modeling pollutant fate, transport, and exposure to connect emissions to air quality, water quality, and population and ecosystem health — extended toward ecosystem services and downscaled planetary boundaries, confronting the open problem of how to share a finite global safe operating space.

3 Artificial Intelligence for Industrial Ecology

Fine-tuning large language models on technical and process-engineering literature to generate and correct inventories (extending LiAISON-AI's RAG procedure), with reinforcement learning for adaptive circular supply chains and physics-informed surrogate models for rapid, uncertainty-aware assessment — lowering the expertise and time rigorous LCA demands.

4 Climate-Aware Capacity Expansion

The integrative thrust. Capacity-expansion models decide what gets built, where, and when over decades — yet optimize on cost and carbon alone. I couple them with the methods above so build-out is evaluated against the full range of life-cycle consequences, closes the loop with circular economy through dynamic MFA, and is climate-aware — optimized for resilience against future weather and extreme events.
